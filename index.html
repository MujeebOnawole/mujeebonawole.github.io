When should we trust an AI prediction?

A model with 90 percent test accuracy still gets one prediction in ten wrong, and its accuracy score does not say which one. For a chemist deciding which compound to make next, or a clinician reading a genetic risk score, that single prediction is what matters.

My research answers the question one prediction at a time. An ensemble of models is asked two things. Do the models agree on the answer? Do they agree on the reason, meaning the atoms, fragments or sequence positions that drive the prediction? The two answers place every prediction in one of four trust scenarios, so a user can act on the reliable ones and send the rest for checking.

Because the framework works on model outputs and attributions rather than on any one kind of data, it carries across scientific domains. In metal-organic frameworks, for example, predictions labelled trustworthy fell within 20 percent error 83.8 percent of the time, compared with 8.3 percent for predictions flagged as low trust. The projects that follow apply the same question to five different areas of science.

Shared method

Antimicrobial discovery

Which molecules will act against priority pathogens, and which fragments explain that activity?

  • Activity models for S. aureus, E. coli and C. albicans at 88–95 percent precision, used to screen about one million compounds.
  • A four-tier test of whether model explanations are chemically valid.
  • Fragment-guided generative design that proposed 700 candidates, 40 percent predicted active.
  • The same pipeline extended to molecular taste (bitter, sweet, tasteless).

Carbon capture and porous materials

Can a model of metal-organic frameworks tell us which of its gas-separation predictions to believe?

  • PoreGCN adds Voronoi pore vertices to atomic graphs of 51,163 frameworks.
  • R² = 0.86 for CO₂/N₂ selectivity, with atom- and pore-level explanations.
  • Trustworthy predictions 83.8 percent accurate at the 20 percent error threshold, against 8.3 percent for flagged cases.
ChemRxiv 2026 App Code

Transition-metal chemistry

Can graph models learn metal chemistry from SMILES and show where each prediction comes from?

  • tmGNN-XAI predicts seven quantum-chemical properties for 100,703 transition-metal complexes, with atom-level trust attribution.
  • CoordBind-XAI predicts metal–ligand binding strength and donor atoms across 61 metals from 19,964 stability constants.
  • Without being given these trends, CoordBind-XAI recovers the Irving–Williams series and the lanthanide contraction.

Genomics

When is a genetic score, or a model of gene regulation, reliable for the individual case in front of us?

  • TrustPGS gives each person a reliability label for their polygenic score when models are transferred across ancestries.
  • RegTrust-XAI applies trust labels to sequence-to-function models in regulatory genomics.

Proteins

Where does a protein property model stop being competent?

  • ProtTrust-XAI maps the competence boundary of a protein property model, with plastic-degrading enzymes as a case study.
  • Residue-level explanations show which parts of the sequence drive each prediction.
